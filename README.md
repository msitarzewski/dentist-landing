# Feast Dental landing page (demo)

**Live:** [the Feast Dental demo](https://msitarzewski.github.io/dentist-landing/), [how it was made](https://msitarzewski.github.io/dentist-landing/its-the-prompt/route/), and [It’s the prompt](https://msitarzewski.github.io/dentist-landing/its-the-prompt/), a page comparing this build with two others made from shorter prompts. Built by an agent team from [Agency Agents](https://github.com/msitarzewski/agency-agents).

What this repository holds:

- The Feast Dental page (`index.html`, `assets/`) and the tools that generate its pictures and bite shapes (`tools/`).
- `its-the-prompt/`: the comparison page. The screenshots of runs 01 and 02 were supplied by their author; run 03 is this page.
- `its-the-prompt/route/`: how run 03 was made, generated by `python3 tools/build-route.py` from `tools/route-template.html`, the specimens and prototypes in this repository, and a curated list of review screenshots (`its-the-prompt/route/evidence.json`), of which only web copies are published.
- The working documents: research (`research/`), brand and specs (`brand/`), every word on the page (`content/copy.md`), prototypes (`prototypes/`).
- The review reports (`.ops/qa/*.md`) and the session log (`.ops/session.jsonl`). The screenshots, traces and logs they cite are not included (about 900 MB), and neither are the screenshots of real practices' websites taken during research (`research/shots/`), which are not ours to redistribute.
- GitHub Pages serves the repository root as is (`.nojekyll`).

A single static landing page for a **placeholder** dental practice. The practice, the dentist, her story,
the prices, hours, phone number and address are invented, and the photographs are AI-generated. The page
says so in a strip above the header and in the footer, and it carries `noindex`. Nothing here may go live
until the section "Before a real practice uses this" has been worked through.

No framework, no build step, no dependencies, no cookies, no analytics and no request to any other host.

## Run it

```sh
python3 -m http.server 4173 --bind 127.0.0.1     # or: npm run preview
# then open http://127.0.0.1:4173/
```

Serve it rather than opening the file: `page.js` is an ES module and browsers will not load modules from
`file://`. The preview server does not compress; a real host should gzip or brotli the HTML, CSS and JS
(about 35 kB of text instead of 139 kB with `gzip -9`, measured 2026-11-10).

## Before a real practice uses this

From `research/04-inclusive-visuals-review.md` section 3.7 and `content/copy.md`. Not legal advice; the
governing rules are the dentist's own state board's.

- **Every picture of the dentist becomes a real photograph of the real dentist**, with her written release
  and no generated, composited or retouched substitute. A generated face beside a real name and licence
  number is a misrepresentation of fact.
- **The storefront, the treatment room, the pad drawing and anything showing the premises or what a
  patient is handed become real photographs.** Each states a fact about the practice.
- The food still lifes state no fact about the practice. They may stay generated if the footer says so,
  or be reshot.
- The second sentence of the bio ("In college I went four years without a cleaning…") is invented. Replace
  it with the real dentist's own story; FAQ answer 3 leans on the same fact.
- Patients appear only as real patients of record with written permission. No generated patients.
- Alt text is rewritten against the real photographs and approved by the person described.
- Every `[confirm]` flag in `content/copy.md` is answered by the practice: the comfort list, "Hablo
  español", the insurance plans, the cancellation policy, whether `$195` is a standing fee (if it is an
  introductory price the page must also state the regular fee, who qualifies and the end date), and the
  written-estimate promise, which the dentist must commit to. The invented values listed under "Unconfirmed
  facts" above read as fact, so they need the same answer.
- **Texas advertising rules.** Drawn from `.ops/qa/compliance.md` section 5 (M1 to M12), which quotes
  22 Tex. Admin. Code ch. 108, subch. E. This is not legal advice: the practice's own counsel decides, and
  nobody has confirmed how the Texas State Board of Dental Examiners applies the font-size sentence of
  §108.55(b) (whether a price numeral counts as "type used for the advertised services"; compliance.md 3.2
  sets out the readings).
  - **Fees (M2, §108.53).** Every advertised fee states the period it is in effect: set the real date in
    place of the invented `June 30, 2027` (and `2027-06-30` in the JSON-LD) and keep it current. A fee that can
    rise is worded as a minimum ("From" is the lowest fee …), and the first-visit condition (more if the gums
    need more than a regular cleaning) stays beside the price. A price chip either says "from $195" or links
    to the full terms. An introductory price also states the usual fee.
  - **The designation (M4, §108.55(b)).** "General Dentist" directly after the dentist's name, in type at least
    as large as the type used for the advertised services. The hero sets it and the "$195" numeral at one size
    (`--fs-lock`); keep them equal.
  - **Owner and licence (M5, §108.52(c)(3), §108.59).** Name the owner prominently, the legal entity behind the
    trade name, and the Texas licence number; the trade name must be on file with the Board. The demo shows
    neither the entity nor a number.
  - **Notices (M6, compliance.md section 7).** HIPAA notice of privacy practices; a website privacy policy (it
    must mention `localStorage.motion`); the Texas notice of electronic disclosure; the Good Faith Estimate
    notice in Cost; an accessibility statement; nondiscrimination and language notices only if the practice
    takes federal funds.
  - **Openings (M3).** Real availability from the scheduler in place of `sampleSlots()`, or delete the
    openings block (`.op`). Never sample times on a live page.
  - **The two flags in `page.js` (M7, M8).** `BOOKING_OPEN_ALL_NIGHT` and `TEXTS_ANSWERED` are both `true` in
    the demo. Set each to `false` unless it is true for that practice: a scheduler that confirms a slot at any
    hour; a text line read in opening hours with an after-hours auto-reply. Without real-time booking, "Book
    online any time" in the ticker goes too.
  - **Insurance card and texting (M7, M11).** Name the channel a card photo goes through (best: an upload in
    the booking tool, under a business associate agreement); if cards or questions come by text, say that texts
    are not encrypted; name the in-network plans; keep "an estimate from your plan, not a promise from your
    insurer".
  - **Spanish and reviews (M9, M10).** "Hablo español" only if the dentist speaks it; "0 reviews" only while
    true, and dated.
  - **Retention (M12, §108.60(a)).** Keep a dated copy of every published version (HTML, images and a full-page
    screenshot) for four years after it last appears.
- The ground and counter colours were sampled from two of the generated portraits so those pictures bleed
  into their fields. New portraits need a backdrop within about 3 levels per channel of `--ground` and
  `--counter`, or the two tokens are re-sampled. `node tools/build-images.mjs` prints each backdrop against its
  field; it fails only if encoding moves a backdrop more than 2 levels from its source PNG, and a source that is
  itself more than 3 levels off is reported, not refused (four probes are, on the current pictures).
- One or two readers from the neighbourhood look at the page before launch.

## Regenerating the generated files

**Pictures** (needs `magick`, `cwebp` and `avifenc` on the PATH; Node 20 or newer; no npm packages):

```sh
node tools/build-images.mjs          # or: npm run images
```

It reads `assets/img/src/*.png`, never modifies them, and writes `assets/img/<name>-<width>.{avif,webp,jpg}`,
the icons (`favicon.svg`, `favicon-96.png`, `-32`, `-16`, `apple-touch-icon.png`), `avatar-400.png`,
`social-card.jpg` and `manifest.json`. Same input, same bytes out. Crops are baked in. Files it no longer makes
are deleted, except that a run without `tools/social-card-type.json` skips the card and the avatar and keeps
their last good copies. The icons and the card draw the bite from `tools/bite.mjs`, so a change to the bite
needs both scripts run. To add or swap a picture follow the header of the script, then run it with
`--markup <file>` to get a paste-ready `<picture>` element; change only its `alt`. Keep the source PNGs: they
carry the generator's provenance data, which resizing strips. Each entry's `slots` and `sizes` describe the
frames as `page.css` draws them; when a layout changes, measure the frames again and update both, or browsers
fetch the wrong width (they were last measured on 2026-11-10).

The hero is art-directed. From 1024 wide the figure shows the whole 4:5 frame (`paloma-hero-640/800/1122`);
below 1024 it is a wide band, filled by a crop of the same picture (`paloma-hero-band-640/800/1122`, source
rows 41 to 757). `index.html` preloads one set per range (`media="(min-width: 1024px)"` and
`media="(max-width: 1023.98px)"`), so each visit fetches exactly one hero file. The crop's top row (41) is the
band's `object-position` y (6% in `page.css`) times the height the crop removes: change one and the other
must follow (the comment on `paloma-hero` in the script has the arithmetic). The drawing beside the pull line has
4:5 files for its 4:5 frame and 3:2 files for the stacked layout; the `media` on its 4:5 sources must be the
width at which `page.css` makes that frame 4:5 (640 since 2026-11-10).

**Lettering on the card and the avatar** (macOS only): the outlines in `tools/social-card-type.json` come from
`tools/social-card-type.swift`, which sets each run's `text` with CoreText. Edit the `text`, then:

```sh
swift tools/social-card-type.swift tools/social-card-type.json \
  Anybody=prototypes/fonts/anybody-latin-wdth-wght.woff2 PublicSans-Medium=/path/to/PublicSans-Medium.ttf
node tools/build-images.mjs
```

Public Sans Medium 1.008 (static TTF, SIL OFL) is not in this repository. With it and the Anybody file above, the
script reproduced the committed outlines byte for byte on 2026-11-10. The card's strip uses the page strip's
wording.

**The bite** (the notch, the three first-visit discs, the crescent):

```sh
node tools/bite.mjs                  # or: npm run bite
```

The geometry is defined once, in `tools/bite.mjs`: `shapes.bite` is the notch from
`brand/02-visual-system.md` 4.1 and `portionBites` places it on the disc (4.3). The script rejects a shape
that would leave stray specks or a disc in two pieces, then rewrites `assets/css/bite.css` (and
`prototypes/proto-bite.css`). `tools/build-images.mjs` imports the same `shapes.bite` for the icons and the
card. Tile sizes are in `page.css`: the tokens `--bite-lg`, `--bite-md` and `--bite-chip` for controls, and
`--notch` on the wordmark plate (45px, 35px on phones) and on a bitten photograph (25% of its width). Each mask
image reaches `REACH` (64) tiles, so a masked element may be up to 63 tiles wide; the widest, the wordmark
plate, is 1905px at a 5000px viewport against a limit of 2835px.

## How the motion is built

Five mechanisms, as `brand/03-interaction-spec.md` defines them: bite, chew, open wide, ticker, accordion.
All of it is opt-in twice: inside `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)` and under
`:root:not([data-motion="off"])`, which the "Stop the motion" control toggles. With motion off, with no
script, and for anything already scrolled past, every state is simply drawn: discs bitten, wide words wide,
the ticker a wrapped list. Colour changes stay on.

Rules the prototypes paid for; keep them when editing:

- A bite is **one** mask layer, parked outside the corner and moved in with no transition. Never
  `mask-composite: exclude`: it leaves a hairline wherever the element's own edge is anti-aliased.
- Never put `border-radius` on the masked box itself: Chrome then draws the mask coarsely. The masked
  `.btn__face` is square and the rounded, outlined pill is its `::before`.
- The focusable element is never masked or transformed, so the focus ring and hit area stay whole.
- `var()` is not resolved in a keyframe's `animation-timing-function` in any engine: springs inside
  `@keyframes` are written out.
- A word that changes width sits in a cell sized by a hidden copy of itself (`.w` with `data-text`); the
  visible text has `contain: inline-size`, so a spring's overshoot never moves its neighbours.
- Once script has called `play()` or `pause()` on a CSS animation, `animation-play-state` no longer
  controls it. The ticker's pause, rest and resume must all go through the same calls.
- A local fallback face has no width axis, so each resting width of Anybody has its own
  metric-matched stand-in: `"Anybody fb"` at the top of `page.css`, and `"Anybody fb 100"`, `75`, `72`, `62`,
  `56`, `50`, `125` and `100n` in the LATE-FONT STAND-INS block at its end. The hero lock-up uses the last
  three: `"Anybody fb 50"` for GENERAL DENTIST, `"Anybody fb 125"` for the numeral from 640 up and
  `"Anybody fb 100n"` below; her name (`.who`, at width 62.5) uses `"Anybody fb 56"`. Anybody is
  `font-display: block`, so during its block period the page is laid out, invisibly, in these faces: they are
  what keeps a late font from moving the hero.
- The footer cookie bites on `click`: a short press or a tap takes one bite when released (WCAG 2.5.2: it can
  still be cancelled by sliding off), and Enter and Space take one bite each. Only a press held 280ms keeps eating, one bite
  every 280ms, on the way down. A keyboard click (`detail` 0) always bites, even after a held press that slid off.
- Give each stand-in `font-weight: 100 900`. A `local()` face without a weight range gets synthetic bold in
  Firefox at the page's 800 and 900, which widens it by about 4.7% and moved the lock-up sideways by up to 27px
  when the font arrived late (`.ops/qa/final-verification.md`).
