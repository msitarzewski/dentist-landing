Four things the lead should know before building on this:

1. **The name check is a web search, not a trademark clearance, and it missed things twice.** Quoted, standard-mode searches returned nothing for "Carrot Dental"; an unquoted extended search then found two live practices. My five searches on "Kernel Dental" found nothing; the independent second pass found a live dental software brand on that domain. "Nothing found" below means exactly that, and the real name needs a proper clearance.
2. **"Feast" needs "Dental" beside it in the first screen.** With food still lifes and a food word, the page could pass for a restaurant in a five-second test. The lock-up is FEAST DENTAL, never FEAST alone, in the header, hero, title tag and footer.
3. **A typographic consequence of the Texas rule.** `02` §6 reports that Texas requires "General Dentist" directly after the dentist's name "in a font no smaller than the largest used for the advertised services". The direction sets service names at headline size. Either the designation is set at display size once (it suits heavy condensed capitals) or service names stay no larger than it. I have not read the rule text myself; confirm against §108.55(b) before the layout is final.
4. **US wording.** The creative direction says "ice lolly". Page copy must say "ice pop" or, in Austin, "paleta". And nothing frozen belongs in the first screen (Part 1).

## 1. Pressure test of the direction

**Verdict: keep it. One rule: the picture bites, the words welcome.**

**Strong.** It sells what teeth are for, a reason to come back that is neither shame nor vanity. It fits Persona A, who usually arrives because something specific stopped working (`02` §3). It gives the brand a tooth-free shape, lets humour reach the layout (`01b` Part 5 #8, #10) and keeps the vocabulary positive (`02` §4e).

**Where it backfires.** (1) Aimed at the patient, "bite" is a threat or a cost: bites back, bite the bullet, a bite out of your wallet. (2) Cold or hard food makes anyone with a sore tooth wince. A bitten ice pop is a pain cue; a crunch is a cracked tooth. (3) A headline about biting leaves belief 1 unsaid. (4) Food beside a dentist can read as a diet lecture on its way.

**How the voice handles it.** The patient does the biting, and only ever bites food. The headline does the welcome; the bite stays visual. Three bite references in the words, in fixed slots (Part 5). Everyday food, nothing frozen in the first screen, no food called good or bad.

### 2d. Cut to three, recommend one

1. **FEAST DENTAL (recommended).** It is the idea's outcome: teeth are for biting, so go and feast. It is the only survivor with energy in it, it is one syllable in capitals, and nothing in it points at the patient. Its two weaknesses are handled by rule: "Dental" is always locked to it, and it is never used as a verb or a pun in copy.
2. **APPETITE DENTAL.** The gentlest of the three and the cleanest result: no use and no neighbours on either pass. Choose it if "Feast" tests as too much for a nervous first-timer. It gives up speed and some punch.
3. **PICNIC DENTAL.** No threat in the word at all, and it sets beautifully in capitals. It is third for two reasons: a picnic is slow where this brand is quick, and a practice called Picnic Point Dental already exists. Use it only if the first two fall at clearance.

### 2e. How to use the name

- **Lock-up:** FEAST DENTAL, same face and weight for both words, on one line or stacked. "Feast" alone appears only in running copy, after the full name is on that screen.
- **The notch:** the wordmark is the first bitten thing on the page (designer to test the top-right corner of the T). It is static.
- **No pictures in it:** no tooth, fork, plate or food drawn into or beside the letters.
- **Not a verb:** never "feast your eyes", "feast on", "a feast of". The name is a name.
- **Search phrase goes in the title tag, not the h1:** `General Dentist in East Austin, TX | Feast Dental` (updated to match the build, 2026-11-09) (the SEO h1 is trait 1 of the generic fingerprint, `01` §2).
- **Never "Paloma Dental"** as a short form: Paloma Dental (Denver) and Paloma Creek Dental (Aubrey, TX) are real practices (https://www.zocdoc.com/practice/paloma-dental-57746, https://www.palomacreekdental.com/meet-the-team/).

## 5. Voice guide (one page)

**Who is speaking.** Paloma, first person singular, to one person. Upfront, quick, funny, kind. Never a spa, never a chain.

### Three traits

| Trait | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| **Upfront.** The number, the time and what happens next, before anyone asks. | "First visit: 75 minutes, $195 without insurance. Exam, X-rays, cleaning, and a plan with prices." | "Affordable, personalized care tailored to your unique smile." |
| **Quick.** Short sentences, verb first. Energy comes from verbs, not punctuation. | "Book online. Two minutes. Pick a time and you're done." | "We invite you to begin your journey by requesting an appointment with our caring team!" |
| **Kind, and funny at my own expense.** The joke is on dentistry or on me, never on your teeth. | "Yes, I'll ask if you floss. No, there's no wrong answer." | "Somebody's been skipping the floss!" |

### Her first person

- "I" for what she does or promises (I look, I draw, I stop). "We" only for the place (we're open till 7:30). Never "our practice", "our patients", "the doctor".
- Contractions. Most sentences under twelve words. "You", never "patients" or "folks". No exclamation marks, no put-on Texan. American English: checkup, neighborhood, ice pop.
- Plain nouns: teeth, mouth, bill, numb, filling. Not oral health, investment, discomfort, restorations.
- **Her promises are literally true.** She will ask when your last visit was and whether you floss, so the page never says "I won't ask". It says there is no wrong answer and no lecture.
- **Name forms.** As a label and wherever services are promoted: "Dr. Paloma Krenek, DDS · General Dentist", the designation directly after the name (`02` §6). In her own sentences: "I'm Paloma."

### The three touchy subjects

| Subject | Principle | Two sample lines |
|---|---|---|
| **Fear** | Normal in one breath, then hand over control. Say what she does, not how it will feel. | "Nervous is normal here. Say so at the door and I slow down." · "Raise a hand and I stop." (updated to match the build, 2026-11-09) |
| **Money** | Number first, then what is in it, then what happens if it is more. | "You get the price in writing before I do anything." · "No insurance? The first visit is $195: exam, X-rays and a cleaning. If your gums need more than that, I show you why and price it first." |
| **Time since the last visit** | Say it once, lightly, then move to today. Never ask for the reason. | "Haven't been in years? I went four without. You're welcome here." (updated to match the build, 2026-11-09) · "I don't need the story. Tell me what's bothering you." |

### Vocabulary

**Use:** book online · call or text · first visit · a look · this week (only when true) · price in writing · written estimate · numb · stop · your pace · General Dentist.

**Banned for compliance** (`02` §6): painless, pain-free, "you won't feel a thing" · best, top, leading, #1, "better than" · guarantee, perfect, flawless · specialist, "cosmetic specialist" · "free" unless nothing is folded in elsewhere · "all insurance" · any count, rating or statistic the public cannot verify · "before it gets worse", countdowns, "limited slots" · any quote not from a patient of record with written permission · any carrier name before it is confirmed.

**Banned for the brand:** reimagined, redesigned, different, "a new kind of" · spa, ritual, self-care, pamper, experience, journey, transformation · "welcome to", "your smile is our priority", state-of-the-art · "judgment-free zone" (three competitors say it, `02` P4; show it with specifics) · "relax", "don't worry", "nothing to be afraid of" · affordable, investment, "starting at just" · neglect, "should have", finally, "never too late", "long overdue" · "Request appointment", "Learn more" · every tooth pun, and "open wide" (the command from the chair; it stays an internal name for a type animation) · food morality: guilty pleasure, treat yourself, cheat · emoji.

### The bite in language: hard limit of three

A **bite reference** is any visible sentence that uses bite, bitten, chew, crunch, chomp, munch or gnaw, or that talks about eating. The page may contain **three**, one per slot:

| Slot | Where | Line |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The statement | Meet the dentist, in her voice, once | "Teeth are for biting. I keep them at it." |
| 2. The question | First visit, step two | "Tell me what you've stopped eating." |
| 3. The send-off | Closing band, after the last button | "Then go bite something." |

- If the footer cookie in `brand/03-interaction-spec.md` §6 keeps a visible "take a bite" label, it spends slot 3 and the send-off goes.
- **Grammar:** in the words, the one who bites is you, and what gets bitten is food. Never the practice, a price, a fear, a bullet, or back. So never "won't bite", "bite the bullet", "a bite out of" a cost, "bite-sized", "sink your teeth into".
- **Buttons, labels and captions stay literal:** "Book online", never "Take a bite"; under a still life, "Apple." (updated to match the build, 2026-11-09)
- **Not counted:** the notch, press and scroll bites, screen-reader status text, and the name Feast (which is never punned on).
- **Food in pictures:** apple, toast, corn on the cob, a peach, a taco. No ice, hard candy or popcorn; nothing frozen in the first screen. No food is good or bad in this voice.

## 6. Hero headlines

All ten land belief 1 (`02` §4a: welcome however long it has been, no lecture). Seven words or fewer, first person or direct address.

| Rank | Headline | Words | Why it is here | Watch |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **I don't care how long it's been.** | 7 | First person, beside her face, in a voice no chain can use. It names the exact thing the visitor is ashamed of and throws it out. Most energy. | Never break the line after "care". Must be followed at once by what she does care about. |
| 2 | **Been a while? Come in. No lecture.** | 7 | The belief, whole, in three beats that stack well in capitals. Safest in a five-second test. | Less ownable: Tend's subhead works the same ground. |
| 3 | **You're not late. You're right on time.** | 7 | Turns the shame over without mentioning it. Warm. | Lowest energy of the top three; reads a little like a poster. |
| 4 | It's been years? I don't keep score. | 7 | First person, and it is a promise about conduct. | |
| 5 | No lecture. Just a look. | 5 | Lowers the stakes of the first visit. | The visit is more than a look; the subhead must say so. |
| 6 | I fix teeth. I don't give lectures. | 7 | Blunt and funny. | "Fix" undersells the cosmetic lane. |
| 7 | Bring the teeth you've got. | 5 | Funny, accepting, memorable. | Says "welcome" by implication only. |
| 8 | Welcome back. No lecture. | 4 | Shortest; treats a stranger as a regular. | "Welcome" is where template sites begin. |
| 9 | Forget last time. Book this one. | 6 | Speaks to a bad past visit (F4) and carries belief 2. | "Last time" is ambiguous. |
| 10 | No lectures. Open till 7:30. | 5 | A joke with a fact in it. | Only on days it is true; depends on the real hours. |

Left out on purpose: "Been a while? Good." (to a visitor who fears being oversold, "good" sounds like a dentist rubbing her hands); "You're not late. You're next." ("you're next" is the moment in the waiting room this audience dreads); "I won't ask where you've been" (she will ask; see the literal-truth rule in Part 5); "No late fees" (reads as a claim about the cancellation policy).

**Subheads for the top three** (one sentence each; the formal label "Dr. Paloma Krenek, DDS · General Dentist" sits with the portrait in every version):

1. *I don't care how long it's been.* → "I care about what's bothering you now. I'm Paloma, a general dentist in East Austin. You get a real look, the price in writing, and no lecture." (updated to match the build, 2026-11-09)
2. *Been a while? Come in. No lecture.* → "I'm Paloma, a general dentist on the east side, and your first visit is 75 minutes with the price in writing before I do anything."
3. *You're not late. You're right on time.* → "Whenever your last visit was, this one starts the same way: a proper look, a cleaning, and a plan with prices, from me, with no lecture."

**Test before launch.** Run 1 against 2 in the five-second test proposed in `02` §7, with at least two people who have not seen a dentist in two years. If anyone reads 1 as cold, ship 2.
